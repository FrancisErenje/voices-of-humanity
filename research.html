Research Centre

Every documentary begins with research. Voices of Humanity documents languages through historical study, cultural research, community knowledge and documentary storytelling.

Why Research Matters

Every language represents generations of accumulated knowledge, identity and history. Accurate documentation requires patience, careful verification and respect for the communities whose stories are being preserved.

Voices of Humanity is committed to responsible documentation by consulting reliable publications, historical references, linguistic research, community knowledge and field observations wherever possible.

Our Documentary Process

📚 Research

Study the language, its speakers, history and current situation.

📝 Script Writing

Transform research into engaging documentary narration.

🖼 Visual Collection

Gather photographs, historical references, maps and cultural imagery.

🎙 Narration

Record narration designed to educate and inspire.

🎬 Production

Combine narration, visuals and editing into a documentary.

🌍 Digital Preservation

Publish documentaries that remain accessible for future generations.

Field Documentation

Voices of Humanity values original field documentation whenever possible. The Ukelle documentary included an on-location interview conducted at Wanokom Market with a Special Assistant to the Local Government Chairman.

The project also preserves the Igede language through an ongoing educational series launched on 26 May 2026, featuring structured Igede language lessons taught by retired secondary school principal and educator Mr. Erenje.

Looking Ahead

Voices of Humanity is building a permanent digital archive of languages, cultures and traditions. Every documentary strengthens this archive and contributes to preserving humanity's shared heritage.

Future plans include documenting additional Nigerian languages, expanding field research, preserving oral traditions and collaborating with communities across Nigeria and eventually beyond.
